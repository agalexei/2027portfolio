NRMA Sum Insured Discovery

I wanted to know what homeowners understood about rebuild costs, and what they could recall after seeing a proposed design.

Year
2026
Role
End-to-end research and design for Sum Insured
Focus
Comparative research, comprehension, confidence, trust

The context

As part of Reimagine, I wanted to find out whether the proposed Sum Insured design made rebuild costs easier for homeowners to understand.

What I needed to learn

Before showing any design, we asked homeowners to explain sum insured in their own words. This gave us a baseline for what they would include in a rebuild estimate and how they might work it out.

How I tested it

I split insured homeowners evenly between the existing design and the proposed minimum viable design. Both groups answered the same questions. I compared ratings and open-text feedback with a short quiz on rebuild costs, inclusions and coverage to see what people could recall accurately.

What the study showed

The proposed design also scored higher on perceived helpfulness (+9.6%) and clarity (+7.8%). Participants retained more accurate information, and the strongest trust gain was in perceived insurer competence (+10%). These were findings from the comparative study. They gave us a direction for the next design iteration: keep what helped people understand the estimate and explain rebuild costs in plainer language.
