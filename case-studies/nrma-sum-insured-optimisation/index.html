NRMA Sum Insured Optimisation

Customers paused after seeing their estimated rebuild cost. I used session replays to understand where the quote flow stalled, then worked with the team to make confirmation clearer. Next-page progression was 14% higher in the four weeks after release than in the previous four.

Where customers paused The hypothesis What we changed Results

Where customers paused

After the redesigned NRMA Home Insurance Quote & Buy journey launched, fallout at the Sum Insured step was about 5% higher than in the previous journey. Customers had already made it deep into the quote. I focused on what happened on that page before assuming the drop-off reflected lower intent.

In Tealeaf session replays, customers often paused after seeing their estimated rebuild cost. They scrolled, hovered and reread sections. Many delayed confirming the estimate even though they rarely changed the amount. That pattern gave us a more specific problem to work on.

Test whether the next step was clear

Our hypothesis was that the connection between Confirm and continuing the quote was too easy to miss. The action existed, but customers had to infer its purpose while reviewing an important cover decision. We expected clearer sequencing to help more customers continue after reviewing the estimate.

Design around the way customers reviewed the estimate

Focus attention
We simplified the colour palette and made the rebuild estimate the visual focal point so the first thing to review was clear.
Put confirmation at the decision point
We moved Confirm to the end of the page, where the replays showed customers finishing their review.
Make progression explicit
Next appeared after customers confirmed the amount, making the relationship between the two actions visible.

What changed after release

In the first four weeks after release, next-page progression was 14% higher and time on the Sum Insured page was 11% lower than in the preceding four weeks. Against a separate baseline, people spent about 20 seconds less per session on the page than in the pre-redesign experience.

Higher progression and shorter page time were consistent with less hesitation. The comparison measured behaviour across two time periods. Customer confidence was an interpretation of the pattern, rather than a measured outcome.
